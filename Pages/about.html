About Ahmedabad

India's first World Heritage City, founded in 1411 on the banks of the Sabarmati.

Founded on the Sabarmati

In 1411 CE, Sultan Ahmad Shah founded a new capital on the eastern bank of the Sabarmati River, naming it after himself. He chose the site for its riverside position and strategic value, laying the first stone of what would become Bhadra Fort — the citadel that still anchors the old city today.

A walled city of pols

As the city grew under successive Sultanate rulers, its residential fabric organised itself into pols — gated clusters of wooden havelis, each usually tied to a particular trade, caste or community. Narrow shaded lanes, shared squares and chabutra bird-towers gave the pols both security and a distinct, still-recognisable character. Many of these wooden houses, several centuries old, remain inhabited today.

Sultanate architecture

Ahmedabad's Sultanate-era builders developed a hybrid architectural language, drawing on Hindu and Jain temple-building traditions alongside Islamic forms. Jama Masjid's 260 carved pillars, and Sidi Saiyyed Mosque's famous stone jali screens, are frequently cited as the finest expressions of this local synthesis — sometimes called Indo-Saracenic or Gujarat Sultanate style.

A multicultural heritage

Over six centuries, Ahmedabad's built heritage absorbed Sultanate, Mughal, Maratha, Jain, Hindu, and colonial-era layers, followed by 20th-century modernism through the work of architects like Le Corbusier. The city's identity as India's textile and trading hub — the "Manchester of the East" — added a further, distinctly mercantile layer to its cultural life.

UNESCO World Heritage inscription

In 2017, the Historic City of Ahmedabad was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List — the first Indian city to receive this designation. The inscription recognised the walled city under UNESCO criteria (ii) and (v): criterion (ii) for the interchange of Hindu, Jain and Islamic architectural values reflected in Sultanate-period buildings, and criterion (v) for the continuously inhabited urban fabric of the pols, still exhibiting traditional ways of life. Ahmedabad is India's first World Heritage City.
